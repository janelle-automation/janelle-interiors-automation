Why it was built

The studio kept its tracking in a spreadsheet somebody had to keep alive, and its chasing in whoever remembered. This replaces both with an intelligence layer over Gmail and Drive — reading project activity, holding one live source of truth, and doing the follow-up and reporting that used to eat a coordinator's week.

Without changing how anyone works: the team stays in Houzz Pro and Gmail.

The reading pass · continuous

From an email to a record

01

Fetch

New mail and watched Drive folders, every ten minutes by default. PDFs downloaded.

02

Classify

Project-relevant, and what is it — a vendor quote, an order confirmation, an approval?

03

Extract

Claude turns it into structured data: project, vendor, item, price, PO number, dates.

04

Resolve

Matched to an existing project, vendor or PO — or flagged as new.

05

Write & raise

Saved, pushed live to the board, and where warranted, a task with an owner and a date.

Anything it is not confident about surfaces in triage for a person to confirm, rather than being guessed at silently.

The follow-up engine · 02:00 nightly

What has gone quiet, late or missing

Vendor silence

Past the studio's threshold, three business days by default.

Approval overdue

A client decision outstanding longer than it should be.

Dates slipping

A delivery or install drifting past, or already past, its date.

Spec gaps

Missing dimensions, finish, SKU or lead time, blocking an order.

For each one, Claude writes the message with the project and contact already in it — and then stops.

A Gmail draft

In the right person's mailbox. Never sent.

A card in the inbox

One click through to the draft in Gmail.

A log entry

So the same nudge is never raised twice.

Features · the assistant

Jenny answers from the records, not from a guess

A personal assistant rather than a page. Anyone can ask her about any project, vendor, order or deadline, and the answer comes from the studio's own data.

A daily briefing

What needs you today, most pressing first, with the task cards attached.

Spoken, hands-free

She reads an answer aloud and listens for the next question, for the drive or the site visit.

Reads what you hand her

Attach a PDF or a photo and ask about it. She can draw a plan or a board, too.

Installs as an app

Jenny has her own address and her own icon — a phone app, separate from the studio system.

Named agents for the jobs the studio repeats — Inbox Triage, Vendor Follow-ups, Project Coordinator, George and Chief of Staff — each with its own brief and its own way of answering.

Where she can act, she still asks first — and anything outbound is still a draft.

Per role and per project

A seat sets the shape of access; project assignment narrows it further.

Enforced at the row

Postgres row-level security, not a check in the interface that could be bypassed.

Google is per person

Each person grants their own Gmail and Drive access, and can withdraw it at any moment.

Tokens are encrypted at rest; scopes are read-and-draft only, never send.

The whole thing, in a paragraph

Mail and Drive are read on the studio's own schedule. Claude classifies each message, links it to a project and a vendor, raises a task with one owner, a date and a next step, and drafts a reply where one is warranted; PDFs become quotes, orders and POs. Every night the engine looks for what has gone quiet, late or malformed and prepares the nudge. Every morning a digest says what is overdue and what needs the principal; every Monday a report says how the week went. Anyone can ask Jenny about any of it and get an answer from the records rather than a guess.

Every outbound message stops in a draft, and a person presses Send.
