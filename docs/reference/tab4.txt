Here’s a polished prompt you can reuse with snapshots of tile, stone, wood, fabric, paint, hardware, plumbing finishes, and other project materials:
INTERIOR DESIGN MATERIALS — EDITORIAL PRESENTATION PROMPT
Act as my Senior Interior Designer, Creative Director, Interior Stylist, and Luxury Architectural Photographer.
I am providing one or more casual snapshot images of materials selected for a residential interior design project.
These may include:
Tile
Natural stone
Countertop slabs
Cabinet finishes
Wood samples
Flooring
Paint colors
Wallpaper
Fabric
Upholstery
Plumbing finishes
Decorative hardware
Metal finishes
Lighting finishes
Rugs
Specialty materials
Your job is to transform these simple material snapshots into a beautiful, sophisticated editorial materials photograph suitable for a high-end interior design presentation.
PRIMARY GOAL
Create an image that feels like it was professionally styled and photographed for:
Architectural Digest
Elle Decor
Luxe Interiors + Design
House Beautiful
A luxury residential interior design studio presentation
Do not make it look like a casual phone photo, sample-board snapshot, retail display, or AI-generated collage.
PRESERVE THE ACTUAL MATERIALS
The supplied samples are the approved or proposed design materials.
Preserve as accurately as possible:
Actual colors
Veining
Grain
Texture
Pattern
Finish
Scale
Material character
Hardware shape
Tile shape
Fabric pattern
Do not redesign the materials or substitute unrelated products.
The finished photograph should still clearly represent the materials I supplied.
COMPOSITION
Arrange the materials into a refined, layered designer flat-lay or editorial vignette.
Create intentional overlap and visual hierarchy.
Use a combination of:
Larger architectural materials as the base
Smaller samples layered naturally
Stone and tile partially overlapping
Wood or cabinetry samples anchoring the composition
Fabric folded or softly draped where appropriate
Hardware or metal finishes used as small accents
Paint samples integrated subtly
Plumbing or decorative finishes positioned elegantly
Avoid perfectly straight rows or a catalog-style grid unless the materials naturally call for it.
The composition should feel curated, effortless, dimensional, and expensive.
PHOTOGRAPHY STYLE
Create the appearance of a professionally photographed material story.
Use:
Soft natural daylight
Warm diffused directional light
Gentle architectural shadows
Realistic material texture
Natural depth of field
Crisp but not overly sharpened details
Accurate whites
Sophisticated neutral color balance
Subtle contrast
High-end editorial styling
Camera perspective should generally be:
Overhead flat-lay or slightly angled 30–45 degree editorial view.
BACKGROUND
Use a quiet luxury background such as:
Warm ivory plaster
Soft limestone
Pale natural oak
Cream linen
Light travertine
Warm neutral microcement
Off-white textured paper
Choose whichever background best complements the supplied materials.
Do not introduce a background color that competes with the design palette.
STYLING
If appropriate, introduce only a few restrained styling elements such as:
Small architectural sketch
Linen swatch
Pencil
Subtle design drawing
Natural stone fragment
Minimal brass ruler
Small organic branch
Interior specification card
Keep styling minimal.
The actual design materials must remain the hero.
Do not add excessive flowers, coffee cups, candles, books, jewelry, or decorative objects that distract from the selections.
MATERIAL REALISM
Make every material look tactile and believable.
Stone should show authentic depth and veining.
Wood should show natural grain.
Fabric should show weave and softness.
Tile should show realistic surface irregularities.
Metal should have realistic reflection without excessive shine.
Matte finishes should remain matte.
Polished finishes should reflect light naturally.
CLEAN UP THE ORIGINAL SNAPSHOTS
Remove or minimize:
Fingers or hands
Messy desks
Packaging
Stickers
Barcodes
Retail labels
Background clutter
Plastic wrapping
Harsh overhead shadows
Phone-camera distortion
Glare
Poor white balance
Distracting sample edges
If a manufacturer label is important for identifying the material, keep it subtle and professionally integrated.
OVERALL AESTHETIC
The final result should feel:
Quiet luxury. Warm. Architectural. Organic. Sophisticated. Layered. Timeless. Residential. High-end. Editorial.
Think:
Luxury interior design studio material palette photographed for a client presentation.
Avoid making the image overly beige or monochromatic if the supplied materials contain meaningful color.
PRESENTATION QUALITY
The finished image should be appropriate for:
Canva client presentation
Interior design moodboard
Finish presentation
Material selection presentation
Design proposal
Project specification book
Instagram or portfolio imagery
Use a clean landscape composition with generous negative space, suitable for placement on a presentation page.
IMPORTANT
Do not invent major new materials.
Do not significantly change the color of approved materials.
Do not make the samples look computer-generated.
Do not create visible text unless it already exists on the sample and is necessary.
Do not turn the image into a traditional moodboard with digital cutouts.
The goal is a realistic, professionally styled photograph of the physical material selections themselves.
FINAL IMAGE DIRECTION
Create one cohesive, magazine-quality editorial photograph that makes these everyday material snapshots look like they were professionally styled and photographed by a luxury interior design studio for a client presentation.
A useful variation is to add the specific room name at the top—such as “Primary Bathroom,” “Kitchen,” or “Exterior Materials”—so the styling can be tailored to that space.
