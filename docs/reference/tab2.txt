Interior Design Presentation Rendering Prompt
Act as a senior interior designer, architectural visualization artist, and luxury hospitality photographer.
I am providing several reference images for a client's interior design project. These may include:
Architectural elevation drawings

Countertop or stone slabs
Tile and backsplash materials
Flooring
Plumbing fixtures
Lighting

Decorative materials
Your job is to combine the elevation and material selections into one highly realistic interior design visualization suitable for a professional client presentation.
PRIORITY #1 — FOLLOW THE ELEVATION
Use the architectural elevation as the primary guide for the design.
Preserve as accurately as possible:
Overall room proportions
Cabinet locations
Cabinet widths and heights
Drawer and door configurations
Appliance locations
Window locations and proportions
Door locations
Hood size and shape
Millwork details
Countertop heights
Backsplash locations
Niches
Shelving
Architectural openings
Symmetry and spacing
Do not redesign the cabinetry or architecture unless I specifically request a change.
If the elevation and inspiration photographs conflict, the elevation takes priority.
MATERIAL APPLICATION
Carefully study the material reference images and apply them realistically.
Match as closely as possible:
Cabinetry
Use the exact cabinet door style, wood tone, paint color, grain direction, panel detailing, fluting, molding and finish shown in the reference.
Countertops / Stone
Use the supplied countertop or slab reference. Preserve the overall color, veining character, movement and scale of the stone.
Where appropriate, create realistic slab placement and bookmatching without making the stone excessively busy.
Backsplash / Tile
Use the supplied tile reference and accurately represent:
Tile size
Color
Finish
Variation
Grout
Handmade texture
Layout pattern
Apply the tile only to the areas indicated by the elevation or my notes.
Hardware
Use hardware that matches the supplied finish and style. Maintain realistic scale and placement.
Walls and Architectural Finishes
Accurately reproduce specified plaster, limewash, paint, stone, wood or specialty wall finishes.
Show subtle natural variation and texture.

CAMERA & COMPOSITION
Create a polished architectural photograph.
Use:
Straight vertical architectural lines
Correct perspective
Eye-level camera position
Approximately 24–35mm architectural lens appearance
Balanced composition
Minimal lens distortion
Natural depth of field
Full visibility of the important design features
For elevations, favor a straight-on composition that clearly communicates the design.
Do not use dramatic wide-angle distortion.
STYLING
Keep the room sophisticated and presentation-ready.
Add only minimal, appropriate styling such as:
A few books
Ceramic vessels
Branches or greenery
Small decorative objects
Simple countertop accessories
Styling should support the architecture, not hide the design.
Do not clutter countertops.
IMPORTANT RESTRICTIONS
Do NOT:
Change the architectural layout
Add extra windows
Remove windows
Add cabinets that are not shown
Change cabinet configurations
Invent additional architectural details
Add artwork where windows are shown
Change selected materials
Substitute a different stone
Change tile color
Change appliances
Add excessive accessories
Over-style the room
Make the image look overly CGI
Make surfaces unnaturally perfect
If a design detail is unclear, maintain the simplest interpretation of the architectural elevation rather than inventing something new.
FINAL IMAGE
Create one high-resolution, photorealistic luxury interior visualization that clearly communicates how the client's completed space will look when constructed.
The final image should feel:
Timeless, refined, warm, sophisticated, architectural, luxurious and believable.
It should be polished enough to place directly into an interior design presentation, moodboard, Canva presentation or client proposal.
